While working as a Senior Prototyper at The Telegraph, I collaborated with product designers and the picture desk to build a scroll-triggered visual storytelling format for long-form journalism. The first piece built with the system, [“A Year in Lockdown”][lockdown], achieved over 100,000 views, a 15-minute average dwell time, and won a Society for News Design (SND) award.

## The Challenge

The Telegraph produces strong photojournalism, but standard article templates treated images as static breaks between paragraphs. For major visual features, the editorial team wanted an immersive, full-screen narrative format where photography, video, and text worked together as the reader scrolled.

The main constraint was the Content Management System (Adobe Experience Manager). Building bespoke, standalone microsites for every visual story took too much developer time and pulled journalists out of their normal publishing workflow. We needed a way for editors to write and manage stories inside the standard CMS while delivering a bespoke interactive experience on the frontend.

## The Stack

- **Vanilla TypeScript** for zero-dependency DOM enhancement and scroll state
- **IntersectionObserver API** for triggering image crossfades and viewport-aware video playback
- **CSS Grid & `position: sticky`** for responsive split-screen, full-bleed, and stacking text layouts
- **esbuild** to bundle the runtime script and CSS into a single self-contained payload
- **FFmpeg & ImageMagick** for batch-optimising desktop/mobile video loops, blurred poster frames, and photography

## The Solution

### Progressive enhancement inside the CMS

Instead of bypassing the CMS, I designed the system as a lightweight script (`ux_scrolling.js`) that progressively enhanced standard long-form articles at runtime.

Journalists wrote copy and uploaded images in the CMS as normal. To turn a section into a scroll-triggered sequence, they placed a small HTML marker at the top of the block with data attributes configuring the layout mode and background colour:

```html
<span
  class="scroll_container"
  data-background="#181818"
  data-layout="scroll--left,scroll--stacking"
></span>
```

When the page loaded (outside of the CMS authoring `iframe`), the script located these markers, stripped out empty `<p>` tags generated by the editor, promoted standard column figures to full-bleed containers, and injected the scroll layout classes and CSS custom properties. If JavaScript failed or was disabled, the article fell back to a readable standard article.

### Tuning the feel with an in-browser editor

Getting the pacing right on a scroll-driven story is hard to do in static design tools. Small changes to scroll distances, transition durations, or easing curves completely change the mood—from slow, sombre crossfades to sharper, high-contrast cuts.

Before building the production CMS script, I created an in-browser prototyping editor so the designer and I could experiment with the feel in real time.

### Sticky layouts and `IntersectionObserver` triggers

Rather than using heavy scroll-jacking libraries that hijack native scrolling, the production system relied on native CSS `position: sticky` combined with `IntersectionObserver`:

- **0px top-edge detection:** By configuring `IntersectionObserver` with `rootMargin: "0px 0px -100% 0px"`, the observer created a single trigger line at the top of the viewport, toggling `.active` classes to crossfade stacked sticky `<figure>` elements cleanly as the reader scrolled.
- **Responsive split-screen & stacking text:** On mobile and narrow screens, text cards floated over full-bleed (`100vw × 100vh`) photography. On wider viewports (`min-width: 1024px` with a `3/4` minimum aspect ratio), CSS Grid and sticky positioning split the screen—pinning photography to 60% of the viewport on the left or right while short lines of text scrolled up and stacked sequentially alongside it (`scroll--stacking`).
- **Viewport-aware video & iOS WebView handling:** A second `IntersectionObserver` (`threshold: 0.5`) automatically played and paused ambient `<video>` loops as they entered and left the screen, swapping in portrait-cropped video sources on mobile devices. The script also detected The Telegraph's native iOS app WebView to work around iOS autoplay restrictions and Safari sticky `z-index` compositing bugs.

### Impact

The format launched with [“A Year in Lockdown”][lockdown], a visual retrospective marking the first anniversary of the UK's Covid-19 lockdown. The piece drew over 100K views with an average dwell time of 15 minutes—well above standard article engagement—and was recognised with an award from the Society for News Design (SND).

## Technical Contributions

- **CMS progressive enhancement:** Built a zero-dependency JavaScript and CSS enhancement layer that transformed standard AEM long-form articles into scroll-driven stories via declarative `data-*` markers
- **Prototyping tooling:** Created an in-browser GUI editor with visual viewport guides to test scroll spacing, typography, and animation curves with designers in real time
- **Scroll & layout architecture:** Implemented native `position: sticky` crossfades, split-screen stacking text layouts, and viewport-triggered video playback using `IntersectionObserver`
- **Cross-platform compatibility & media pipeline:** Handled iOS Safari/WebView quirks, dynamic polyfill loading for older browsers, and wrote FFmpeg/ImageMagick shell scripts to compress responsive video and photo assets
